# Browser and performance review (2026-11-17)

The current candidate prepares current-mall models during startup, deduplicating shared files, reporting failures and retaining available procedural stand-ins. The loader provides a directory exit, an enter-now choice and a bounded wait. Prepared means downloaded and parsed, not GPU-ready: first use can still upload geometry or compile shaders. Separate product-preview contexts are not included. The combined candidate on `ef50fa3` passed nine Chrome Metal cases covering success, missing and corrupt assets, timeout, early entry, directory, context loss and the overall wait limit. In the 30-second timeout fixture the loader released after about 30.8 seconds with incomplete models accurately reported as pending.

Auto samples eligible foreground frame intervals and adapts between Low and Balanced with warmup and hysteresis. Explicit selections and recovery remain independent. Detailed is available manually but automatic promotion is disabled until its draw budget is verified. This is a partial implementation of the requested best-quality behavior; neither 60 FPS nor a 30 FPS minimum can be guaranteed. A local 1440×900 Metal walking sample on an earlier scene reported roughly 60 FPS in Auto and Low, but did not establish improvement on weaker devices. Cold GPU work can still cause long frames.

The original three shop doors use proximity opening; fourteen additional walk-in stores retain their existing automatic doors. Isolated Chrome/Metal tests on the earlier scene covered entry and exit at all 17 stores and representative desktop/mobile door cases. Physical devices remain untested.

The prior strict V11 suite observed a Detailed-mode frame above 200 calls; experiments to omit glow or combine transparent shadows did not establish a safe general fix. S04 mobile framing was also observed clipped in an earlier layout. A mobile Lighthouse snapshot on an earlier candidate scored 94 accessibility, 100 best practices and 100 SEO, and its viewport disallowed zoom. The draw-budget and S04 framing findings require revalidation on the final build. Native install dialogs, physical iOS/Safari and screen readers were not tested.

The `93b5774` integration passed nine real Chrome Metal preload scenarios, including success, failures, early entry, directory and context recovery. The 30-second bounded-wait fixture released after 30.328 seconds with 3 of 14 shop jobs settled. A separate Chrome check confirmed that slow frames while the loader is visible or models are still preparing do not trigger an Auto downgrade; sampling resumes after preparation settles. The 17-store check exercised physical entry and exit, and four focused door groups passed on this integration. Before the later NPC continuity edit, the `249cf11` candidate had byte-identical V11 JavaScript runtime files except for the regenerated scoped PWA worker; `npcs.js` has since changed. These checks do not establish a hardware FPS guarantee. The strict `93b5774` V11 run failed the 200-call cap at the New Moon Detailed view with measured frames of 205 and 213 calls; a separate `249cf11` investigation observed 214. The overrun persisted with no rendered NPCs and remains unresolved.

A seated NPC previously vanished after 18 seconds while still in view. The timer-based removal is removed, and a 25-second execution regression checks the same seated instance before exercising player-priority seat release. The 249 candidate also passed the real-model Chrome Metal facing/clearance check (four walkers, 43.15 metres, no intersections). Distance unloading, draw-budget visibility changes and player-priority seat release can still make NPCs disappear; this is not proof that every apparent teleport is resolved.

The V11 page and gallery template now permit browser zoom while retaining scene-specific touch handling. In an isolated generated build, Chrome DevTools mobile Lighthouse passed the `meta-viewport` audit on both V11 and the gallery. At 390 × 844, the V11 Tools/Help and gallery Settings panels opened without horizontal page overflow; the scene retained `touch-action: none` while the page body remained `auto`. A Chrome mobile-emulated touch pinch increased the gallery visual viewport scale from 1.0 to approximately 2.0. This is not a physical-device or iOS/Safari result. The same Lighthouse runs reported V11 cumulative layout shift of 0.151 and a gallery site-identity accessible-name mismatch; those findings remain open.

A real Chrome Metal seated-instance probe retained the same seat and coordinates for 25 simulated seconds. Its stronger initial-visibility assertion failed because the selected NPC was not admitted by the current render budget (`visible=false`); the retained-instance test therefore does not verify on-screen continuity. Investigate and retest NPC visibility together with the scene draw-budget overrun.
