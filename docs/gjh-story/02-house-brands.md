# New Moon and the Goh Joo Hin house brands

**CONFIRMED** means an official source or two independent sources support the stated fact. For conflicting company histories, it confirms what each source says. **REPORTED** means one non-official source supports the claim. **UNVERIFIED** marks an open question.

## New Moon from tin to reunion table

**CONFIRMED.** New Moon dates its establishment and first product, abalone, to 1959. Singapore’s IPOS entry T5924841J for canned abalone gives 23 April 1959 as the filing date and 8 March 1960 as the registration date, with Goh Joo Hin Pte Ltd (GJH) listed as proprietor. GJH’s corporate timeline puts the first house brand under 1959, while its company-attributed SG60 account places New Moon’s entry into the portfolio in the mid-1980s. [New Moon history][newmoon-history] · [IPOS record][tm-newmoon] · [Corporate timeline][timeline] · [SG60 account][sg60]

**REPORTED.** A sponsored 2023 Vulcan Post interview describes an acquisition in the 1980s. **UNVERIFIED.** The original owner, exact transfer date and source of abalone in the first tins remain open. The available trademark record and histories leave these details unresolved. [Sponsored interview][vulcan] · [IPOS record][tm-newmoon] · [New Moon history][newmoon-history]

**REPORTED.** A December 1999 *Xin Ming Ri Bao* food feature presented New Moon abalone for the millennium festivities. A February 2010 *Lianhe Zaobao* item featured a New Moon abalone product marketed as Chilean. **CONFIRMED.** In 2006 the Singapore Manufacturers’ Federation named GJH’s New Moon Abalone Yusheng its Best Entry for Commercial Sales and Display in the Singapore Packaging Star Award. The brand’s own timeline also dates its move into bird’s nest to 2004, fragrant rice to 2006 and oils and sauces to 2007. [NewspaperSG, 1999][xinming] · [NewspaperSG, 2010][zaobao] · [Award organiser, p. 6][smf] · [New Moon history][newmoon-history]

**CONFIRMED.** GJH’s eShop names four origins on specific products: New Zealand and Australia on separate 425 g wild-caught tins, South Africa on a 425 g tin, and Mexico on a 454 g tin. The company presents New Zealand as the flagship and Mexico as its most exclusive offering. Its range also includes products made elsewhere: a 2009 Agri-Food & Veterinary Authority notice named GJH as the importer of a specific 400 g New Moon broth from Thailand. [New Zealand][nz] · [Australia][au] · [South Africa][za] · [Mexico][mx] · [Brand positioning][newmoon] · [AVA notice][ava]

## Building the portfolio and protecting the marks

**CONFIRMED.** GJH dates MILI, its second house brand, to 1981. Its Singapore Class 29 canned-food mark T8101608Z was filed on 9 April 1981. MILI grew around everyday ingredients, including canned produce, meat, seafood and sauces. The Health Promotion Board lists two MILI oyster sauces under GJH. Trademark records document a stylised MILI mark owned by GJH in Hong Kong (19882721) and a US application GJH filed in 2013 showing a cartoon letter M beneath the wordmark. [Corporate timeline][timeline] · [IPOS record][tm-mili] · [MILI profile][mili] · [HPB supplier entry][hpb] · [Hong Kong IP Journal][hk] · [USPTO Gazette, TM 269][us-mili]

**CONFIRMED.** GJH’s timeline puts GOGO and Flying Wheel together under its 1982 export milestone. The brand pages give 1986 for GOGO and 2004 for Flying Wheel. The GOGO page traces a move from canned drinks for export to retail *potong* ice cream, and Singapore mark T8300573E shows a 1983 filing and 1986 registration in Class 29. Flying Wheel’s later Singapore device mark 40202522044V covers specified goods in Classes 29, 30 and 32. Its braised abalone eShop listing describes Japanese-species abalone and gives China as the product origin. **UNVERIFIED.** The individual founding years remain unresolved. [Corporate timeline][timeline] · [GOGO profile][gogo] · [Flying Wheel profile][flying] · [GOGO mark][tm-gogo] · [Flying Wheel mark][tm-flying] · [Braised abalone listing][fw-tin]

**CONFIRMED.** GJH calls Borsch Med its fifth brand and dates its brand registration and Pien Tze Huang distributorship milestone to 2004. A 2009 US Gazette entry names Borsch Med Pte. Ltd. as applicant for its own bilingual logo and cites three Singapore registrations dated 18 November 2003. The sources leave the connection between those earlier marks and the 2004 milestone open. Zhangzhou Pien Tze Huang Pharmaceutical names itself as manufacturer and marketing authorisation holder for its 3 g product. **UNVERIFIED.** Current exclusivity and the terms of the Singapore distributorship remain open. [Corporate timeline][timeline] · [Borsch Med history][borsch] · [USPTO Gazette, TM 36][us-borsch] · [Manufacturer’s product record][pzh]

**CONFIRMED.** Borsch Med described itself in a 2024 announcement as operated and managed by GJH staff. The same announcement names a separate authorised distribution arrangement with Japan’s Zeria Pharmaceutical. [Borsch Med’s 2024 announcement][borsch-release]

**CONFIRMED.** Myanmar’s IP journal records GJH’s New Moon crescent-and-face logo application MM/T/2020/013332. The UK IPO records a different portfolio effort: GJH applied for NoNoMeat for meat substitutes in 2021 and successfully defended the application against an opposition in 2022. **UNVERIFIED.** The decision leaves the retail launch date open. [Myanmar IP Journal][my-newmoon] · [UK IPO decision, paragraphs 1 and 38][uk-nono]

**Do not dramatise**

- A named founder signing the 1959 application, a dated acquisition handshake, or the first abalone harvest. [IPOS record][tm-newmoon] · [SG60 account][sg60]
- A single founding year for either export brand, or a Japanese harvest scene based on species wording alone. [Corporate timeline][timeline] · [GOGO profile][gogo] · [Flying Wheel profile][flying] · [Braised abalone listing][fw-tin]
- Pien Tze Huang as a GJH invention, a cure scene, or an exclusive distribution contract. [Borsch Med history][borsch] · [Manufacturer’s product record][pzh]
