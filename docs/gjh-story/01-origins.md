# Goh Joo Hin’s origins on Rochor Road

**Evidence labels.** **CONFIRMED** means an official source or two independent sources support the stated claim. **REPORTED** means a detail rests on a single non-official account. **UNVERIFIED** marks an unresolved claim or a gap in the records reviewed. When an account conflicts with another, the label applies to the attributed statement.

## The provision store and the two founding accounts

**CONFIRMED — GJH’s account.** Goh Joo Hin traces its business to a provision store on Rochor Road in Singapore in the 1940s. Its corporate timeline credits the Goh brothers and says the shop supplied nearby households with basic food items. The SG60 feature also describes a family-run business that developed a distribution network. GJH wrote that feature, so it carries the same company perspective. [GJH corporate timeline](https://www.gjhglobal.com/about-us/) · [GJH’s SG60 feature in The Edge Singapore](https://www.theedgesingapore.com/SG60/heritage/tradition-inspires-tomorrow)

**CONFIRMED — the takeover account.** A 2024 *Lianhe Zaobao* profile based on an interview with second-generation chairman Goh Hock Siang identifies his father, Goh Yeow Gek (吴耀玉), as the man who took over a small shop called 裕兴. The family added its surname to make 吴裕兴. *Vulcan Post* also describes the takeover and renaming and says relatives joined Yeow Gek later. Both accounts describe growth from retail to import-export. [Lianhe Zaobao interview, 16 August 2024](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/singapore/story20240816-4481552) · [Vulcan Post family-business feature](https://vulcanpost.com/851082/family-run-business-singapore-chinese-new-year-delicacies-2024/)

**REPORTED — details of the earlier trade.** The *Zaobao* profile says Yeow Gek came from Chaozhou and sold vegetables near Beach Road. *Vulcan Post* gives “Joo Hin Wholesalers” as the earlier English trading name. [Zaobao interview](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/singapore/story20240816-4481552) · [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/851082/family-run-business-singapore-chinese-new-year-delicacies-2024/)

**CONFIRMED — conflicting published dates.** The 2024 *Zaobao* profile dates the business to **1940**, and a GJH advertisement in the Singapore Kwong Wai Siew Peck San Theng anniversary volume uses the line “Since 1940.” A Dawu Village history supplied by Chaozhou’s municipal civilisation office names Yeow Gek’s brother as 吴松俊, originally 吴耀金, and credits the pair with starting the vegetable business 吴南兴 in **1940** and the canned-goods and grain business 吴裕兴 in **1955**. **UNVERIFIED — which event each date represents.** The accounts leave open the exact opening, takeover and renaming dates and whether the village history’s 1955 date refers to a later phase of trade. Give the shop scene the decade and attribute it to GJH. [Zaobao interview](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/singapore/story20240816-4481552) · [Peck San Theng anniversary volume, GJH advertisement](https://sgpecksantheng.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/%E7%A2%A7%E5%B1%B1%E4%BA%AD140%E5%91%A8%E5%B9%B4%E5%88%8A.pdf#search=New%20Moon) · [Dawu Village history](https://news.southcn.com/m/node_1e8b2d3f2e/29ea361a30.shtml)

## Names and records after the shop era

- **CONFIRMED — family succession.** Goh Hock Siang was the second-generation chairman and Yeow Gek’s son. Goh Kai Kui was named as third-generation CEO in 2026 coverage and as Yeow Gek’s grandson in earlier coverage. Malaysia’s trademark journal prints “GOH JOO HIN @ GOH KAI KIA” alongside Yeow Gek under the trading name GOH JOO HIN, and Singapore’s provision suppliers’ association pairs **Goh Kai Kia** with **吴皆佳**, the chairman’s name in *Zaobao*. The trading-name entry alone cannot identify an original founder. [AsiaOne obituary coverage](https://www.asiaone.com/singapore/goh-hock-siang-chairman-new-moon-abalone-singapore-distributor-dies) · [Zaobao obituary](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/singapore/story20260813-9516010) · [Vulcan Post](https://vulcanpost.com/851082/family-run-business-singapore-chinese-new-year-delicacies-2024/) · [Malaysia IP Official Journal, p. 304, entry 157](https://www.intellect-worldwide.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/123/2023/07/13-APRIL-2023-BATCH-12-2023.pdf) · [Provision Suppliers & Merchants Association directory](https://psma.org.sg/membership.php)
- **REPORTED — later street address.** Registry mirrors show a separate business entry, **GOH JOO HIN**, UEN **00906100B**, registered **25 October 1975** and listing **244 Rochor Road**. Both mirrors reflect registry data. **UNVERIFIED —** the 1940s shop number and any formal transfer from this registration to the current company. [SGPBusiness record](https://www.sgpbusiness.com/company/Goh-Joo-Hin) · [RecordOwl record](https://recordowl.com/company/goh-joo-hin)

The filtered ACRA record was unavailable during this pass. ## Singapore shop context

**CONFIRMED — contextual references.** The National Heritage Board documents rice and cooking oil as provision-shop staples. Its booklet reproduces a 1950 photograph of a shopkeeper weighing goods with a *dae*. It also profiles Hock Lam Aik Kee, which sold dried goods and animal feed in 1930s Jalan Kayu and let farmers buy on interest-free credit. The National Library Board’s later Sago Street photograph shows small packets and goods set out by a shophouse’s five-foot way. These are references for period retail and visual props. [NHB, *Traditional Provision Shops*, pp. 3, 16–19](https://www.nhb.gov.sg/~/media/nhb/files/resources/publications/ebooks/nhb_ebook_traditional_provision_shops.pdf) · [NLB Sago Street photograph, c. 1980](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/image-detail?cmsuuid=f62d301a-8a18-4f78-ab6b-53be13d91845)

**CONFIRMED — 1940s city context.** The National Archives holds a 1950 aerial photomap made for postwar planning. NLB documents food shortages during the Japanese Occupation and rice imports remaining below prewar levels until 1947. **UNVERIFIED —** GJH’s exact inventory, layout and wartime operations. [NAS 1950 photomap](https://corporate.nas.gov.sg/discover-archives/media/aerial-photomap-singapore-town/) · [NLB postwar food history](https://biblioasia.nlb.gov.sg/all-sections/vol-15-issue-1-apr-jun-2019-feeding-the-hungry/) · [GJH timeline](https://www.gjhglobal.com/about-us/)
